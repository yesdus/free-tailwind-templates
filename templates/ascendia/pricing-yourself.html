Charge for the outcome, not the hour

Hourly rates cap what you can earn and punish you for getting faster. Here is the script our readers used to move to project pricing.

By Marisol Vega

Leah had been a designer for nine years when she realised the better she got, the less she earned. Projects that once took her forty hours now took twenty-five, and her invoices shrank with them.

So she stopped quoting hours. For her next three proposals she wrote down the result the client wanted, what it was worth to them and three options at different scopes. Two clients picked the middle one.

“The client was never buying my hours. They were buying the launch date.”

Our script has four steps: name the outcome, put a number on its value, offer three packages and stay quiet after you say the price. The silence is the hardest part.

Not every client will follow you. Leah lost one. Her income rose by forty percent in the same number of working days, and she now uses our day-rate calculator before every quote.

The takeaway

  • Write the outcome in the client's words first
  • Always offer three scopes, not one price
  • Use the day-rate calculator as your floor
