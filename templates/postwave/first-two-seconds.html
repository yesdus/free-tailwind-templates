Hooks that hold: what the first two seconds really decide

We studied 4,000 short videos to see what keeps people watching past the opening frame. The answer is less about shock and more about a question.

By Mina Sato

Viewers decide in about two seconds whether to keep watching. Loud openings help a little; open questions help a lot.

Open a loop

Videos that started with a promise — “this mistake costs you half your views” — held 41% more viewers at the five-second mark than videos that started with a greeting.

The loop has to close, though. Clips that never paid off the promise saw comments turn negative fast.

“A good hook isn't a scream. It's an unanswered question the viewer feels they can solve by staying.”

Move in the first frame

A cut, a hand entering frame, a zoom: any motion in the opening frame beat a static shot. Even talking-head creators gained retention by starting mid-gesture.

Caption the hook

Most people start with the sound off. Hooks shown as on-screen text in the first second outperformed spoken-only hooks in every category we tested.
