Salt

What My Grandmother Salted

Essay · 7 minutes

Kitchen

She measured nothing and wasted nothing, and she believed a recipe written down was a recipe already half lost.

My grandmother salted the water, the cod, the doorstep in January and, once, a letter from the bank. Salt was how she told a thing it was hers to deal with.

She kept it in a blue tin with no lid and took it by the fistful. When I asked how much, she looked at me the way you would look at someone asking how much air to breathe.

I have tried to cook her soup from memory for eleven years. It is always nearly right, which is a different thing from right and much sadder.

“A recipe written down is a recipe already half lost.”

Last winter I finally wrote it down, against her rule. Four lines, no quantities. Then I put a fistful of salt in the water and stopped measuring.

It was the closest I have come. I think the missing ingredient all along was not asking how much.
