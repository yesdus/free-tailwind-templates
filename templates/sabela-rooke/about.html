About

Essayist, slow novelist and keeper of too many notebooks. I live two streets from the harbour I write about.

Sabela

At the reservoir, pretending not to be photographed.

I grew up above a chandlery, where the shop bell rang every time somebody needed rope, and I have been listening at doors ever since.

I studied marine biology, badly, and left to work nights at a ferry terminal, where I filled the notebooks that became my first book. Since then I have written two more, taught for a few winters, and learned to gut a fish without looking.

My essays are mostly about small places and the long memories they keep. I write in the mornings, by hand, and type it up when I can no longer read it.

This site is where the shorter pieces live. If one of them stays with you, write and tell me. I answer everything, slowly.

Sabela Rooke is the author of Low Water, The Salt Ledger and Small Hours. Her essays about tidal towns, family kitchens and inherited objects have appeared in print and on the radio. She lives two streets from the harbour she writes about.

Sabela Rooke grew up above a chandlery on the north coast. After leaving a degree in marine biology she worked nights at a ferry terminal, where she kept the notebooks published as Small Hours. Her novel The Salt Ledger followed, and her essay collection Low Water was shortlisted for the Harbour Prize. Her essays about tidal towns, family kitchens and inherited objects have appeared in print and on the radio. She teaches a short winter course on the personal essay and lives two streets from the harbour she writes about.
