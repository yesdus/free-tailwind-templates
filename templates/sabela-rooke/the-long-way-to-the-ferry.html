The Ferry

The Long Way to the Ferry

Essay · 9 minutes

Estuary

The boat across the estuary takes eleven minutes. For thirty years my family has left the house an hour early to catch it.

Nobody in my family has ever missed the ferry, and nobody has ever arrived on time for it either. We arrive early, absurdly, and stand on the jetty with our bags at our feet as if the boat might leave out of spite.

My father calls this being ready. My mother calls it your father. I have come to think of it as the family's one real ritual, more reliable than Christmas and observed with more feeling.

The hour on the jetty is where everything gets said. Engagements have been announced there, and one bankruptcy. It is hard to storm off when the only way out is a boat that has not arrived.

“It is hard to storm off when the only way out is a boat that has not arrived.”

When I moved away I started catching trains with two minutes to spare, and felt modern for about a year. Then I noticed I had stopped telling anyone anything.

So now I go early again. I stand on platforms like a woman waiting for a ferry, and sometimes, if I am lucky, somebody stands beside me and starts to talk.
