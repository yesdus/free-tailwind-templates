The quiet cost of reorganising every eighteen months

Nadia HaddadPartner, Organisation

Every reorganisation arrives with the same promise: fewer layers, faster decisions, clearer ownership. And for a few months it often delivers. The trouble starts when the next one is announced before the last one has settled.

In the companies we work with, the average leadership team has redrawn its structure every eighteen months over the last five years. Each change looked sensible on its own. Together they taught managers one lesson: wait, because the chart will change again.

Where the cost hides

The visible cost is consulting fees and severance. The invisible cost is far larger: projects that pause while sponsors change, customers who lose their contact, and knowledge that walks out with the people who were moved sideways.

“Structure is the slowest tool a leader has. Decision rights are the fastest.”

What to change instead

  • Write down the twenty decisions that matter and name one owner for each.
  • Change the meeting rhythm before you change the boxes.
  • Promise the organisation a period of stability, and keep it.

Structure still matters. But it should be the last lever pulled, not the first.
